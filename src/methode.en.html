The idea

Mioto ranks the __N_VERSIONS__ versions of __N_MODELS__ new-car models sold in France according to your criteria. You choose what matters to you (budget, body style, powertrain, range, boot space…) and how important each criterion is; every version then gets a match score out of 100.

How the score is calculated

Each criterion you switch on is scored out of 100. For numerical criteria (price, boot, energy cost, range…), just meeting your target earns 80; doing clearly better earns up to 20 extra points, falling short loses points. Scores are weighted by the importance you choose (secondary ×1, important ×2, essential ×3) and brought back to a score out of 100. Price is set as a range (optional minimum, maximum). It is treated more strictly than the other criteria once the maximum is exceeded: its score drops to 0 at 15 % over (for example €34,500 for a €30,000 maximum). Below the minimum, the score falls more gently and reaches 0 at 30 % under.

When two cars have the same score, the cheaper one comes first, then the one with the lowest energy cost, the longest electric range, the most power, and finally a French brand ahead of a foreign one. A missing figure counts as 0.5.

A failed “essential” criterion can hide the car from the ranking (option switched on by default).

The data

  • Prices: “from” price of the entry-level trim for each powertrain, taken from French manufacturer price lists.
  • 2026 malus: the malus is a French registration tax on new cars. Mioto estimates the CO₂ malus (2026 scale, from €50 at 108 g/km up to €80,000) and the weight malus (from 1,500 kg, €10 to €30 per kg depending on the band, with the allowances for hybrids; electric cars are exempt), capped together at €80,000. By default, the Price criterion compares prices including the malus; you can switch this off. Allowances for large families or disability are not taken into account.
  • Consumption and electric range: lowest figures announced by the manufacturer on the combined WLTP cycle.
  • Energy cost: estimate based on WLTP consumption, your annual mileage and the energy prices you enter, excluding charging losses and real-world driving.
  • Purchase incentives: new electric car that has obtained the French environmental score, under €47,000 and 2.4 t (the CEE “coup de pouce” incentive, formerly the ecological bonus). Check the exact version on the ADEME website before buying.
  • Safety: the model's Euro NCAP rating when it has been tested; an untested model gets an average score on this criterion.

The database is maintained by hand by the editor and republished automatically every night, after consistency checks. Spotted a mistake? Report it via the Legal notice page.

The reviews

Each model page includes an “Our verdict” review: verdict, strengths, weaknesses, who it suits and useful information (warranty, facelift, announced successor…). These reviews summarise at least two road tests published by different motoring media, three in the vast majority of cases. Every strength or weakness appears in at least one of those tests; when tests contradict each other, the point is qualified or removed. The reviews were written in French and translated into English.

Reviews are never part of the score: it depends only on your criteria and the figures.

Independence

Mioto has no link with any manufacturer or dealer, earns no commission and shows no advertising. Links to manufacturers' websites are provided for convenience.

Why “Mioto”?

The name blends “My Auto” and “Miata”, the name of the Mazda MX-5 in the US market. The first-generation roadster, launched in 1989, is the site author's favourite car. It is also where the logo comes from: the two “o”s in Mioto are raised pop-up headlights, like those of that roadster and many sports cars of the 1980s and 1990s.
